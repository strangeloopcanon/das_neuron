Six independent growth economies produce six distinct consumption responses. A neural circuit stores them as six directions in two dimensions and recovers each response exactly when one sector changes.

This is a designed example of superposition. The circuit compresses solved economic responses. The separate policy networks learn economic decision rules from data.

Equations supply the data

Each household owns its sector capital and has logarithmic utility. Production uses capital with full depreciation. There are no transfers between sectors. At capital k = 1, productivity z = 1 is the baseline and z = 2 is the selected shock.

max E Σₜ βᵗ log cᵢ,ₜ
cᵢ + k′ᵢ = zᵢ kᵢα
k′ᵢ = αβ zᵢ kᵢα
cᵢ = (1 − αβ) zᵢ kᵢα
xᵢ = (cᵢ − cᵢ,baseline) / cᵢ,baseline = zᵢ − 1
α = 0.36; β = 0.96

The six normalised response features are known because the economic solution defines them. A singleton shock changes exactly one sector. The circuit encodes that sector's response as one of six directions.

The entire circuit

For one active feature, its direction has dot product 1 with itself. Adjacent directions have dot product ½. The decoder keeps the first response and sets all other responses to zero. All six features use the same two coordinates.

Sharing creates interference

Select opposite directions. Their latent vectors cancel. The decoder sees the same zero vector as the baseline, although two economic responses are present. Select adjacent directions: both reconstructed responses become twice their true value. The economic teacher stays additive; the errors come from the compressed circuit.

The guarantee covers the baseline and six unit-amplitude singleton responses. Multiple shocks stress the representation outside that support. There is no claim of exact recovery at arbitrary shock amplitudes.
